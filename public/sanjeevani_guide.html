1. Problem Statement & System Overview

Over 70% of India's rural population resides in villages where specialized medical infrastructure is virtually absent. Frontline health workers (ASHAs and ANMs) are burdened with paper registers, leading to fragmented patient histories, delayed emergency referrals, and high maternal-infant mortality.

Sanjeevani 4.0 solves this by establishing a digital continuity chain from the rural citizen's doorstep to the Sub-centre, Primary Health Centre (PHC), and District Hospital. It equips frontline workers with offline-resilient digital triage tools, connects patients to doctors via WebRTC teleconsultation, formats verifiable ABDM e-Prescriptions, and provides an AI voice assistant in regional languages (Hindi & Marathi).

Key Innovation: Closed-loop care continuity. Unlike traditional telemedicine where care terminates after the video call, Sanjeevani 4.0 automatically dispatches medications to the pharmacy and assigns scheduled home visit follow-ups to the patient's village ASHA worker.

3. The Five Role Perspectives

Sanjeevani 4.0 features strictly segregated role-based portals configured for every tier of public healthcare:

ROLE 1 Citizen / Patient Portal
View official 14-digit ABHA Card, track multi-stage referral journey, access the ABHA Digital Health Locker to upload/view lab reports, view digital e-Prescriptions, tap 24x7 emergency 108 speed-dial, and converse with Sanjeevani AI in Marathi, Hindi, or English.
ROLE 2 ASHA Frontline Worker Portal
Doorstep patient enrollment, vitals screening (BP, Pulse, SpO2, Temp), automated red-flag triage, one-tap teleconsultation escalation, offline queue reconciliation, and assigned home follow-up checklist.
ROLE 3 ANM (Auxiliary Nurse Midwife) Portal
Sub-centre command overview, high-risk pregnancy radar (ANC schedules, Hemoglobin, TT immunizations), referral triage validation, and sub-centre essential drug stock register.
ROLE 4 Medical Officer / Doctor Portal
Live triage teleconsultation queue, patient longitudinal electronic medical record (EMR), live WebRTC video consultation room, and structured digital prescription generator with automated ASHA follow-up assignment.
ROLE 5 District Chief Medical Officer (Admin) Portal
District-wide GIS epidemiological heatmap, real-time bed & ICU occupancy radar, 108 ambulance dispatch status, immutable ABDM audit trail, and automated one-click PDF/Excel governance reports.

4. Clinical & Medical Safety Q&A

Q1 How are clinical triage levels (Green, Yellow, Red) computed?
Triage is determined deterministically by combining vitals thresholds with acute symptoms:
  • Red (Emergency): Systolic BP ≥ 160 mmHg, Diastolic BP ≥ 100 mmHg, SpO2 < 92%, acute chest pain, active bleeding during pregnancy, or fever with altered mental status.
  • Yellow (High / Moderate Urgency): Systolic BP 140–159 mmHg, SpO2 92–94%, blood sugar > 200 mg/dL, or high-risk third-trimester pregnancy.
  • Green (Routine): Stable vitals (BP < 130/85, SpO2 ≥ 97%) scheduled for standard preventative wellness.
Q2 What happens if a newly registered patient asks the AI about medicines before a doctor consultation?
In Sanjeevani 4.0, the system checks whether a valid completed referral outcome exists. If no doctor consultation has taken place:
  • The AI never recites hardcoded medicines (e.g. Amlodipine or Calcium).
  • It clearly states: "Your clinical consultation is pending with the Medical Officer. No medicines have been prescribed yet."
  • It presents an action button: "Start Doctor Teleconsult" or "Call ASHA Worker".
  • The E-Prescription modal displays an "Initial Clinical Registration Slip" with status "Doctor Consultation Pending / डॉक्टर तपासणी प्रतीक्षेत".
Q3 How does Sanjeevani prevent dangerous medical hallucinations?
The system uses a dual-engine clinical safety model:
  1. Cloud Gemini AI operates under strict system guardrails forbidding direct prescription of Schedule-H drugs, antibiotics, or steroids without a licensed doctor's sign-off.
  2. If cloud API latency exceeds 1.2s or encounters an error, the platform instantly fails over to an internal, hardcoded clinical rule engine.
  3. Emergency keywords (e.g., severe chest pain, unconsciousness, heavy bleeding) bypass conversation and immediately display 108 emergency ambulance and ASHA speed dials.

5. AI Voice & Multilingual Architecture Q&A

Q4 How does the multilingual voice assistant function on rural low-bandwidth devices?
The voice subsystem is split into three decoupled components:
  • Speech-to-Text (STT): Uses the browser's native Web Speech Recognition API with regional BCP-47 tags (mr-IN for Marathi, hi-IN for Hindi, en-IN for Indian English).
  • Clinical Intent Parsing: Queries are mapped against active patient vitals, registered conditions, and referral states.
  • Speech Synthesis (TTS): Text is sanitized by stripping asterisks and markdown, normalized to Devanagari numerals where appropriate, and played back using natural Indian accented voices (e.g., Google हिन्दी, Google मराठी).
Q5 Can an illiterate villager who cannot read English or Devanagari use the system?
Yes. Non-literate citizens can tap the large microphone button and speak in conversational Marathi or Hindi. The voice assistant speaks the answer aloud. Furthermore, the E-Prescription card includes a "Listen to Prescription (ऐका)" button that reads out medication schedules, plus visual sun/moon icons showing morning/night timings.

6. Technical, Database & Offline-First Q&A

Q6 What database does Sanjeevani 4.0 use, and why was it chosen?
Sanjeevani 4.0 uses SQLite 3 (via Node.js native node:sqlite module) storing all records in data/sanjeevani.db.

Why SQLite?
  • Zero-Configuration Edge Deployment: Operates as a self-contained database file with no external daemon, making it ideal for rural PHC edge servers prone to power outages.
  • Full ACID Transactions: Guarantees zero data loss or partial writes during sudden power failures.
  • High Performance: Delivers sub-millisecond query execution on low-cost hardware.
Q7 How does the Offline-First Synchronization work in areas with zero cell connectivity?
When network connectivity drops:
  1. The app switches to offline cache mode, storing registrations and vitals in localStorage.
  2. The UI displays an amber badge: "Offline Mode — X records queued for sync".
  3. When the ASHA returns to cell coverage, clicking "Sync Records" posts the queued records to /api/sync/batch.
  4. The SQLite database reconciles the records atomically and appends an audit log entry.
Q8 How does Medical Document Upload and the ABHA Health Locker work?
Citizens and health workers can upload PDF documents or photos (JPG, PNG, WebP) up to 10MB, or tap "Scan with Camera" to capture photos of prescriptions or lab slips directly via their device camera. Uploaded records are encrypted, categorized (Lab Report, Ultrasound, X-Ray, Past Rx), stored by patientId, and viewable in full resolution with one-click print and download capabilities.

7. Tough Evaluator & Viva Defense Questions

DEFENSE 1 "What if an ASHA worker enters wrong vitals (e.g. 250 mmHg BP) by mistake?"
The platform incorporates a dual barrier:
  1. Client-Side Range Sanitization: Form inputs enforce physiological boundaries (e.g., BP 50–260 mmHg) and prompt confirmation on extreme values.
  2. Clinical Verification by ANM / Doctor: Emergency red referrals require the receiving Medical Officer to verify vitals and optionally request a live video re-check before issuing medication.
DEFENSE 2 "How is patient privacy protected under Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM)?"
Sanjeevani implements the ABDM Consent Artifact Framework:
  • Patients hold explicit control through a Give / Revoke Consent toggle.
  • When consent is revoked, external doctors cannot access historical charts.
  • Every access, consult, and referral change is logged in an immutable, tamper-evident audit trail with actor designation and timestamps.
DEFENSE 3 "What stops patients from generating fake prescriptions or altering drug dosages?"
Prescriptions are generated exclusively from the authenticated Doctor role session, cryptographically hashed with a UUID tied to the doctor's registration number, and stored server-side. The patient's portal renders a read-only view. Pharmacists verify the prescription ID against the central server before dispensing drugs.

9. Strategic Future Scope & Product Roadmap

Phase 1: Near-Term Enhancements (Next 3–6 Months)

1. Bluetooth BLE Smart Diagnostic Kits: Direct wireless pairing with digital BP cuffs, pulse oximeters, and glucometers via Web Bluetooth API to eliminate manual data-entry errors.
2. WhatsApp / SMS Prescription Delivery: Automatic delivery of bilingual e-Prescription cards to simple feature phones via ABDM SMS Gateway.

Phase 2: Medium-Term Horizon (6–12 Months)

1. 100% On-Device Edge Small Language Models (SLM): Quantized offline LLMs (e.g. Gemma 2B or Whisper Small) running on rural Android tablets for offline voice clinical reasoning in remote tribal valleys.
2. AI Epidemiological Outbreak Radar: Geospatial cluster detection alerting District Health Officers of localized spikes in dengue, malaria, or waterborne diarrhea before an epidemic spreads.

Phase 3: Long-Term Horizon (12–24 Months)

1. Medical Drone Corridor Dispatch: Integration with state drone corridors to dispatch critical anti-snake venoms, maternal hemorrhage kits, and emergency insulin directly to remote Sub-centres within 20 minutes.
2. National ABDM M3 Interoperability: Seamless bidirectional medical record exchange with tertiary AIIMS hospitals and nationwide healthcare networks.
